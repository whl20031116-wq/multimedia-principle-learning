The goal is not to decorate a lesson. It is to help learners make sense of the content.
01 / Understand the idea

What does the principle mean?

Mayer's Multimedia Principle suggests that learners can understand an explanation better when relevant words and pictures work together than when the same explanation is presented with words alone.

Aa

Words explain

Written text or spoken narration can name concepts, describe causes and explain what happens.

◇

Pictures show

Diagrams, illustrations and animations can make structures, sequences and relationships easier to see.

↔

Learners connect

Learning involves actively connecting the verbal explanation with the visual representation—not just looking or listening.

Dual channels & limited capacity

People process verbal and pictorial information through related but distinct systems, and each has limited capacity. A design should not overload either one.

Active processing

Learners select important information, organise it into a mental model, and integrate the models with each other and with what they already know.

03 / Think critically

Boundary conditions

The Multimedia Principle is a design guideline, not a promise that every picture improves learning. Open each point to explore when the benefit may be smaller or when the design needs extra care.

It dependsNovices may benefit from visuals that make unfamiliar relationships visible. Learners who already know the topic may gain less from an additional diagram, especially if it merely repeats what they know. Consider what learners already understand.
Relevance mattersA picture should explain a concept, reveal a relationship or show a process. Decorative images can divert attention; an inaccurate or ambiguous image can create a misconception. Choose visuals for what they teach, not just for appearance.
Keep it manageableA dense diagram can be harder to understand than a clear verbal explanation. Simplify the visual, label important parts, guide attention and reveal a complex process in stages when needed.
Design inclusivelyDo not rely on colour alone, provide meaningful text alternatives for important visuals, use readable labels and consider language level and accessibility. Some learners may need a different or additional way to access the same idea.
Remember: The claim is not “more media is always better.” It is that words and relevant pictures can support deeper learning when they are designed to work together and suit the learner and task.

Key takeaway

Design for sense-making, not decoration.

Choose visuals that teach something, keep them manageable, and help learners connect what they read or hear with what they see. Then ask a question that makes them use that connection.
